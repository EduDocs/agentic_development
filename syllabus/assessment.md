# Assessment

> Why this looks unusual: in an agentic-development course a traditional sit-down exam tests the
> wrong thing. Assessment is **distributed** across four components (D-014); the capstone is the
> summative (D-012). Weights set at S5 (D-024).

## Components & weights

| # | Component | Weight | What it measures | Source | Individual / team |
|---|-----------|:------:|------------------|--------|-------------------|
| 1 | **Presentations** | **20%** | Conceptual grasp — teach it *and* field questions beyond your script | Tutorials: pre-shared md + talk + open Q&A | Individual or pair |
| 2 | **Quizzes** | **20%** | Do you *hold* the concepts (esp. prompt/context engineering)? | Periodic, throughout | Individual |
| 3 | **Challenges (comparative)** | **20%** | How good are your agent / SR outputs — by rubric + leaderboard vs. peers? | Challenges C1–C4 | Mixed (C1 individual → C4 team) |
| 4 | **Capstone Project** | **40%** | Can you do real agentic / evolutionary research in your own domain? | The Project (the final, D-012) | Team or individual |

Weights sum to 100%. Rationale (from the S4 matrix): the **capstone is the dominant single weight**
(40% — sole grader of LO6 evolve + LO7 transfer); the **three supporting components are weighted
equally at 20%** — keeping the Challenge leaderboard from dominating, giving **quizzes** real stakes
as the primary check on the conceptual vocabulary (LO2), and honoring the **broadened presentations**
(LO2 / LO8 / LO9).

## Policies

- **Individual vs team.** Quizzes and presentations are individual (presentations may be pairs).
  Challenges scale from individual (C1) toward team (C4); the capstone may be team or individual.
  Team work also carries the Teamwork rubric (`../assessment/`).
- **Quiz cadence.** Short, frequent concept checks tied to the week's reading and tutorials —
  low-stakes individually, non-trivial in aggregate.
- **Comparative → grade.** The leaderboard *informs* but does not *solely determine* the Challenge
  grade: the per-Challenge rubric (S9) weighs correctness, reproducibility, and honest analysis
  alongside standing. *"Honesty is graded"* — a well-diagnosed miss can outscore a flashy, leaky win.
- **AI-use disclosure (LO9).** The course *is* about using AI, so its use is expected — but every
  submission **discloses what AI did and where**, and the capstone rubric scores critical, honest
  use explicitly (judge / verify / disclose / name failure modes). Framed as transparency and
  critical thinking, **not ethics** (D-022).
