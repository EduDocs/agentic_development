# Capstone Project — Agentic Evolutionary Research in Your Own Domain

**Weeks:** ~9–15 (launches at the pivot) · **Mode:** team or individual (set at S9) · **The
summative (D-012).**

## What it is
The capstone is the course's final and its whole point: take the stack you built across the
foundations — **drive → build → ground → orchestrate → evolve** — and turn it on a real problem in
**your own research domain**. You will build an agentic evolutionary search that **discovers a model
from data in your field**, and report it like a paper. It is due in finals week (week 15) and serves
as the final exam.

## The arc: build → evolve → write up
1. **Build.** Stand up the substrate for *your* problem — a `(features → target)` dataset (or another
   automatically-scorable artifact) from your domain — and **fitness = your Challenge 2 eval
   harness**, adapted. Reuse Challenge 3 grounding (your domain's priors/literature) and Challenge 4
   orchestration.
2. **Evolve.** Add a mutation operator and run an evolutionary search (the [pivot
   module](evolution-pivot.md)). Manage fitness design, diversity, explore/exploit, cost, and
   lineage. Beat your own orchestrated baseline — or diagnose precisely why you couldn't.
3. **Write up.** A paper-style report: problem, method, results, analysis, honest limitations.

## Deliverables
- A **proposal** (~week 9, from [`proposal-template.md`](proposal-template.md)) — scope, data,
  fitness, feasibility.
- A **working system** on GitHub: the evolutionary search — reproducible, logged, with lineage.
- A **paper-style report** ([`report-template.md`](report-template.md)) — IEEE-style, ~8 pages.
- A **final presentation** (also feeds the Presentations component, D-014).
- Leaderboard results where applicable.

## How it's graded
Per [`rubric.md`](rubric.md): the Project rubric (problem formulation, analysis, originality,
organization, presentation) **plus an evolutionary-method dimension**, and the Teamwork rubric. As
the summative it carries the largest assessment weight (set at S5).

## Why your own domain
The foundations used a shared substrate so the cohort could compare. The capstone makes the
**transfer** explicit and real: the methods are domain-agnostic, but the judgment of *what* to
model, *what* fitness means, and *what* counts as a discovery is yours. That transfer is the
degree-level skill this course exists to build.
