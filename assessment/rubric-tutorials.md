# Tutorial / Presentation Rubric

> The **Presentations** assessment component (D-014), **broadened** (D-023) so it certifies
> *conceptual grasp*, not just delivery. Ties to **LO8** (teach + communicate), **LO2** (conceptual
> vocabulary), and **LO9** (honesty about the limits of one's understanding). Per-criterion scales
> and weights are set with the overall scheme (S5).

**Deliverable:** a **tutorial markdown file shared with the cohort ahead of time**, plus a
~15-minute talk (ideally with a live demo) and **open Q&A** in which the presenter fields questions
that go *beyond* the prepared material.

## Criteria

- **Conceptual mastery** *(primary)* — The topic is understood deeply and presented accurately and
  coherently: key ideas, trade-offs, and failure modes — not just definitions.
- **Question handling (beyond the script)** — The presenter answers audience questions that go past
  the prepared talk, reasons on their feet, and says **"I don't know"** honestly at the edge of
  their understanding (LO9). This is the strongest signal of real grasp.
- **The written artifact** — The pre-shared markdown file is accurate, clear, and useful as a
  standing reference, and follows the tutorial shape (what it is · why it matters · key ideas · demo
  · connections · further reading).
- **Teaching & communication** — Clear structure, good pacing, an effective demo, genuine engagement
  of the audience (LO8).
- **Connection to the course** — The topic is tied to agentic development and, where relevant, to a
  Challenge, the capstone, or a spine.

## Notes
- Sharing the md **ahead of time** is what makes the Q&A meaningful: peers arrive having read it and
  can probe for depth. A talk that merely re-reads the file scores low on **Question handling**.
- An honest *"I don't know — but here's how I'd find out"* is graded **well**, not penalized (LO9).
